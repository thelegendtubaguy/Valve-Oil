# Valve Oil UI

[← Back to the README](../README.md)

Valve Oil UI is this project's own web interface for the printer, in Valve Oil's look: dark, with amber for what to press, as on the USB installer's screens. It does everything Fluidd does on these printers, plus Valve Oil's own screens, and sits alongside Fluidd at `http://<your printer's ip>/valve-oil/`. Fluidd stays the printer's main page and doesn't change, and both can be open at once. The two share their settings on the printer: macro categories, temperature presets, cameras, console history and filters, file list choices, and G-code preview options changed in one show in the other.

## What's in it

The screens are along the left, or along the bottom on a phone with the rest under **More**.

- **Home** shows the print in progress, with its thumbnail, time left and when it'll finish, **Pause**, **Tune**, and **Cancel**, which asks first when tapped and cancels straight away when held. **Tune** sets the heater targets, speed, flow, Z offset, and fans during the print. **Objects** lists the print's objects on a map of the bed and skips one, and **Pause at layer** pauses at a layer you pick, on printers whose macros support it. While printing, the camera card switches between the camera and **Layers**, the file's current layer from the G-code preview. A print QIDI's power-loss resume restarted runs from QIDI's copy of the file, so Home finds the file of the same name in the G-code files for **Layers**, the thumbnail, and **Print again**, and says so when it isn't there. With more than one camera, a camera's name picks which one it shows, and **Side by side** shows two. Home also has the toolhead's moves, homing, **Motors off**, extruding, and tuning; every heater's temperature, target, power, and the time until it reaches its target, with its last 10 minutes as a small chart, and every sensor, grouped by Box with each Box's humidity; **Chart**, or a heater's small chart, opens the full temperature chart, where any heater or sensor can be shown and heater power is drawn under the temperatures; the fans, lights, and output pins; recent files with how their last print went; and the macros and a console, folded until opened. In a browser window 1600 pixels wide or more it's four columns, narrower than that two, as on a tablet, and on a phone one. In two columns the camera spans the screen and stops at about half the window's height, and under it the Box, the toolhead, the print, and the macros stack down the left, and the fans and lights, the temperatures, recent files, and the console down the right, each card straight under the one above.
  - **The Box card**, with a QIDI Box connected, shows each slot's spool in its color with its material, brand, and whether it's loaded, and the filament path in one row: the spool feeding it, the hub, and the toolhead sensor, each lit when its sensor sees filament, with a glow running from the spool to each point the filament reaches. From the top, it shows the filament path, then the slot you picked with its buttons, then every Box's slots and the external spool. It has Load, Unload, Eject, Cut, moving a slot's filament in and out, each Box's heater (**Dry** runs QIDI's timed drying, which turns the spools; it needs no print running and no filament in any slot of that Box, neither at the slot's sensor nor loaded to the toolhead, with the spools left on the rollers; **Heat** holds a temperature until it's turned off, within the loaded filaments' Box heating limits), tool mapping, filament editing, [writing spool tags](qidi-box.md#writing-spool-tags), Box settings including **Use QIDI Box**, which chooses whether prints load from the Box or use the external spool, **Detection on insertion**, which also reaches RS-485 Boxes as QIDI's touchscreen setting does, and [turning spools while the Box heats](qidi-box.md#turning-spools-while-the-box-heats), and each printer's load and unload speeds. While a load or unload runs it says what the Box is doing, and when a step fails it shows QIDI's error with **Retry** and **Stop**. The spool button opens the **Filament library**, which edits QIDI's filament list, the one the Box and the slicers' filament matching use. With [Spoolman](spoolman.md) set up, Home shows the spool in use and each slot's spool, and **Change spool** gives the loaded slot its spool, as Fluidd's Spoolman card does.
  - **The External spool card** takes the Box card's place on a printer with no QIDI Box. It shows the external spool's filament, which you change by clicking its name, and whether it's loaded to the toolhead, with **Load**, **Unload**, and **Cut**, which load and unload as the touchscreen does. **Load** heats the nozzle for the spool's filament and waits for you to push the filament into the toolhead; once the extruder grips it, it loads and purges at the chute. **Unload** heats the nozzle, cuts the filament, and pulls it back out of the extruder. **Load** is there while the toolhead's filament sensor detects nothing, **Unload** while it detects filament, and both while a print is paused, to change a spool that ran out.
  - <a id="the-rgb-light"></a>**The Fans and lights card** keeps the printer's switches, such as the case light, the polar cooler, and the beeper, and each light's power along its top. Under them, **Fans** has a slider for each fan you can set, with a menu of Off, 25, 50, 75, and 100% on its value, a target for each temperature fan, and the speed and RPM of the fans Klipper runs itself. **Light control** has each light's color and brightness. On the Max 4, Plus 5, and Smart 5 it also picks the RGB light's effect (Solid, Breathe, Flow, Rainbow, Comet, Spread, or Flash) with its speed and direction, or **Printer status**, QIDI's own light, which breathes while idle and fills with the print's progress. Any other effect turns QIDI's status light off, so it lasts through prints, until Klipper restarts or you pick **Printer status** again. The row of lights over the effects shows roughly what the light is doing. Slicer G-code can set the same effects with [`VO_LIGHT_EFFECT`](settings.md#commands). Both tabs are the same height, so switching doesn't move the page.
- **Files** has the G-code files and the printer's configuration, plus Klipper's config examples and docs to read. It has folders, a grid or list view with sortable columns, search, uploads of files and folders by dragging them in, and for each file: print, add to the job queue, preview, edit, rename, duplicate, move, download, and delete, also for several files at once. Starting a print with a QIDI Box connected first matches each filament in the file to a loaded slot, as Fluidd's Box print dialog does.
  - **The editor** highlights Klipper's configuration, has search and replace, and **Save and restart**, which restarts Klipper, or Moonraker for `moonraker.conf`. It asks before closing with unsaved changes. `fluidd.cfg` opens read-only.
  - **Preview** draws a G-code file layer by layer, colored by tool, feature, speed, or fan, with travel moves and retractions if you want them, and follows the print while it runs.
- **Control** moves the toolhead, homes each axis, extrudes and retracts, and sets the Z offset, which **Save** keeps, pressure advance, speed, and flow. It sets every heater's target within its limits, runs PID calibration, applies temperature presets, and sets the fans, lights, and output pins. **Macros** runs Klipper macros, asking for their parameters, grouped as in Fluidd. **Limits** sets the speed and acceleration limits and switches the runout sensors.
- **Console** sends G-code and shows Klipper's replies, with errors in red. It completes commands with Tab, recalls earlier ones with the arrow keys, lists every command with its help, and has Fluidd's filters. It works while Klipper is stopped, to read why.
- **Tune** shows the bed mesh in 3D or as a heat map, with its range and the probe points that needed a retry, and loads, saves, removes, and calibrates mesh profiles. The mesh's corners are named as you face the printer, front left to back right; X0 Y0 is the front left on every supported printer. In 3D a small printer in the corner turns with the bed, its front marked, so you can tell which side faces you. **Bed soak** runs the [bed soak test](tools.md#bed-soak-test) and charts its runs, as Fluidd's Bed soak card does. **Calibrate** has what the printer has: Z tilt, the bed screws, endstops, probe accuracy and calibration, the load-cell check, and input shaper calibration.
- **History** lists past prints with their details and statistics, and prints one again. **Queue** is Moonraker's job queue, and **Timelapse** has the timelapse settings, render progress, and the rendered videos to play or download.
- **System** shows the printer's computer, each board's load and firmware, and live charts, including Klipper's main thread. The pulse icon at the top of every screen opens the same readings in brief, and turns orange or red when the Klipper or host load, memory, free space, or an MCU's load is high. **Services** restarts, stops, and starts each service, including `qidi-client`, QIDI's touchscreen app, and reboots or shuts down the printer. **Logs** shows and downloads the logs. **Printer screen** shows the touchscreen, as Fluidd's Printer screen card does. **Diagnostics** checks the printer's logs for known faults (see [Diagnostics](#diagnostics)), browses everything Klipper reports, and charts what you pick.
- **Settings** has the printer's name, the language, the theme, jog and extrude defaults, cameras, temperature presets, file and preview options, timelapse, [Add-ons](addons.md), and **Klipper**, which chooses [QIDI's Klipper or Valve Oil's](klipper.md) and shows which one runs and, when Valve Oil's is chosen but QIDI's runs, why.
- **Updates** shows the Valve Oil release, the printer's QIDI firmware, and each board's firmware, what's waiting to install, the **Automatic updates** switch, each install's steps as it runs, and the installer's logs, installs updates with **Update now**, and uninstalls Valve Oil with **Uninstall**. See [Updates](#updates).
- Across the top: the printer's state, its temperatures, **Save config** when Klipper has changes waiting for `SAVE_CONFIG`, notifications (Klipper's and Moonraker's warnings and Moonraker's announcements), the light or dark switch, and **Emergency stop**, which asks first when **Require confirm on Emergency Stop** is on in Settings, as in Fluidd.
- Printer warnings show at the top of every screen, as in Fluidd. A Valve Oil update warning has **Details**, which opens the Updates screen, and a board Valve Oil doesn't support yet has **Open a support issue** and **Details**.
- Opening or reloading Valve Oil UI shows the printer as this browser last saw it straight away, and Klipper's live state takes over when it arrives, about a second and a half later on a Max 4; buttons that need Klipper wait until it's ready.
- When Klipper stops or restarts, every screen stays as it was, with the camera running and the last values Klipper reported, as in Fluidd. A strip across the top says why, in the first line of Klipper's own words, with **Show all** for the rest, and **Firmware restart** and **Restart Klipper**, and links to the configuration and the logs, which still work, as does the console.

## Resuming after a power cut

When the power goes off during a print, Home offers to resume it once the printer is back up, as the touchscreen's **Power loss detected. Resume printing?** prompt does. The print card says **Print interrupted**, with the file and the layer it stopped at. It's offered when the touchscreen would offer it. The last print in the history was cut off before it finished, it had got past its first layer, and the printer isn't printing or paused.

- **Resume print** asks first, then heats the printer up and carries on from that layer, from QIDI's copy of the file, starting a little before where the power went off. Before you confirm, check that the part is still on the bed and nothing is in the toolhead's way. Klipper refuses it while Valve Oil's installer or a QIDI firmware update runs, or while a Box dries, and Valve Oil UI shows why.
- **Discard** asks first, then marks the print cancelled in the history, so neither Valve Oil UI nor the touchscreen offers it again.

The touchscreen can still show its own prompt after you've answered in Valve Oil UI. After resuming, tapping **Cancel** there does no harm, and **Confirm** is refused because the print is already running. After discarding, tap **Cancel** to clear it.

## Language

Valve Oil UI is in English, Spanish, and Russian. It starts in your browser's language when it has it, and in English otherwise. **Settings → General → Language** picks another, kept in that browser, so each computer or phone can have its own. Fluidd's language is set in Fluidd.

Only Valve Oil UI's own text changes language. What the printer writes stays in English: Klipper's and Moonraker's messages, the console, printer warnings, QIDI Box errors, and the update and install messages from Valve Oil's services. Names stay as they are in every language: macro and command names, file names, and Valve Oil, Klipper, QIDI Box, and the other product names.

Corrections and new languages are welcome as pull requests; see [`ui/README.md`](../ui/README.md#translations).

## Updates

The **Updates** screen has everything the **Valve Oil** section of Fluidd's Settings page has, so use whichever is open. Only Valve Oil UI shows an install's steps and the installer's logs.

- **What's installed:** the Valve Oil release, marked **Dev build** with its commit for a dev prerelease, the printer's QIDI firmware, and the firmware this Valve Oil release updates it to when the printer isn't on it yet. **Boards** lists the THR, the mainboard, and each USB Box, with its firmware, whose build it is, and whether it's up to date. On the Max 4 it also lists the closed-loop motor drivers once the printer is on this release's QIDI firmware.
- **What's waiting:** a new Valve Oil release, QIDI firmware, or board firmware, or Valve Oil itself when its files on the printer are missing or changed and it needs to be installed again. With automatic updates on, the screen says whether it installs at the next update check, and around what time that check is due, or what's stopping it, such as a print running or a heater on. With them off, it says to install it with **Update now** or by running the Valve Oil installer. A board whose new firmware needs the newer QIDI firmware says it's updated right after the QIDI firmware, in the same update.
- **Automatic updates** switches whether Valve Oil installs what's waiting by itself while the printer is idle. Valve Oil checks for updates either way. It's the same setting as the switch in Fluidd. If it can't be changed, the switch turns back and the screen says why.
- **Update window** sets the hours automatic updates start in: **Any time**, or **Set hours** with a start and end hour, then **Save**. It names the timezone the hours are read in and where it came from, and offers **Use <timezone>** when your browser's is different. Saving hours on a printer with no timezone saved saves your browser's with them. While automatic updates are on and the printer is outside the window, the screen says the update will be installed during the window. It's the same setting as in Fluidd. See [Update window](install.md#update-window).
- **Update now** asks first, then installs everything that's waiting. When QIDI firmware or board firmware is included, the printer can't be used until the update finishes, which can take a while, and the page loses contact with the printer for part of that time. Don't turn the printer off until it finishes. **Update now** is offered only when something is waiting or an update needs finishing. When the printer can't start it, such as while printing, with a heater on, while another update or update check runs, or while an earlier update needs recovering, it's unavailable and says why, naming what to run for a recovery, and if the printer turns it down after it's pressed, the screen says why.
- **While an update runs,** the screen shows the step it's on, and **Show log** shows its output. A banner at the top of every other screen says Valve Oil is updating the printer and not to turn it off, and stays there while the printer restarts its services and Valve Oil UI can't reach it. Klipper's own warning that an update is running is left out while the banner shows. When Valve Oil UI can't reach the update status but Klipper has been running for a minute without an update running, the banner goes away: the update has stopped or finished, and a printer warning says when it was interrupted. Klipper's restart buttons are hidden until the update finishes. The Updates screen says it's waiting for the printer and reconnects by itself, also after a reload.
- **An install, however it was started,** over SSH, from a USB drive, or by automatic updates, shows on the Updates screen as it runs, as on the touchscreen: each step, the one it's on and what it's doing, how many steps are left, how long it has run, how it was started, and the versions it goes from and to. The banner on other screens names the step. An install that didn't finish stays on the screen for a day, with **View log**, until you close it.
- **Installer logs** lists the log of each of the last ten installs, updates, uninstalls, and backup restores, newest first, to view or download, and says how the last install was started and how it ended. See [Logs](install.md#logs).
- **When it's done,** the result stays on the screen until you close it, with the log open if it failed. A result that says the update finished clears by itself a day after it finished. An update that stopped part way says where it stopped, and **Update now** finishes it. A result closed here is closed in Fluidd too.
- **The last check for updates** says when it ran, and when it failed, why. A dev prerelease's check fails until a release publishes what it looks for, so a dev build doesn't say so.
- **Report a problem** downloads a support bundle of the printer's logs, says to attach it, and then opens a GitHub bug report with the printer's details filled in. See [Reporting a problem](troubleshooting.md#reporting-a-problem).
- **Uninstall** uninstalls Valve Oil, as the installer's [uninstall](install.md#uninstall) does over SSH, without the questions. It asks first, with **Also undo the OS optimizations**, on unless you turn it off; when undoing them needs a restart, the printer restarts by itself when the uninstall finishes. It runs the uninstall from `~/valve-oil/`, so the installed release uninstalls itself. While it runs, Valve Oil UI shows only the uninstall and not to turn the printer off. Once Valve Oil UI has been removed from the printer, it says Valve Oil is uninstalled, with **Open Fluidd**; the uninstall's log is in Fluidd's **Logs**. An uninstall that stopped shows why, with its output, and **Close** goes back to the page. It's unavailable, and says why, while printing, with a heater on, while an update, update check, or install runs, while a QIDI firmware update hasn't finished, or while a board flash or an earlier install needs recovering. Uninstalling needs the printer's `sudo` password to be QIDI's own, as automatic updates do; when it isn't, uninstall over SSH. Fluidd's **Valve Oil** settings don't have it.
- **A QIDI firmware update that failed part way** says it stopped, at which step, and what the printer reported, in full, including any command it names to run first. **Continue the update** carries it on, as does running the Valve Oil installer. While a board flash needs recovering first, it says what to run and waits.
- **A board Valve Oil doesn't support yet,** such as a Box with a bootloader or firmware it doesn't recognize, says what that means for the printer and asks you to open a support issue. **Open a support issue** opens a GitHub issue with the printer, its QIDI firmware, and the board's report filled in; the form also asks how the board got its firmware. **Download the report** saves the board's report, to attach when the form says it was too long for the link. For a Box on firmware QIDI's firmware doesn't carry, it also says to update that Box from the touchscreen's firmware page first. A Box put back on QIDI's firmware stays on it until the next Valve Oil release. When a QIDI firmware update left the Max 4's closed-loop motor drivers on their firmware, the screen says why and to update them from the touchscreen's firmware page, and **Report this board** opens an issue.

The screen checks the printer every 10 seconds while it's open, and every 2 seconds while an update runs. Valve Oil UI also checks once when it opens and whenever it loses the printer, so it notices an automatic update that has started. The printer warnings at the top also say when an update is waiting.

## Diagnostics

**System → Diagnostics → Fault check** reads the printer's logs, `klippy.log` and the older ones beside it, for the faults Valve Oil knows of. For each one it finds, it says what happened, what it means, and what to do, with **Read more** for the docs, how many times it was seen and when, and the log lines that showed it under **log lines**. The check runs at low priority, so it doesn't slow a print. It reads up to a set size of log within a set time, newest first, then stops.

Pick how far back to look, then press **Check the logs**:

- **Since Klipper started:** from Klipper's last start.
- **Last 24 hours:** the last day of the log.
- **Whole log:** all of the logs, as far back as the check can read. This is the default.

The screen always says how far back the check reached, in the printer's time. On a large log it reads only the newest part and says so. With nothing found, it says no known faults were found.

Each finding is marked:

- **Needs attention:** it happened, and the Valve Oil installed now doesn't fix it. Do what it says.
- **Fixed by the release installed now:** it happened on an earlier release, and the one installed now fixes it.
- **Valve Oil repaired it:** each time it happened, Valve Oil put it right before it failed anything.
- **For reference:** worth knowing when reporting a problem, such as QIDI's error codes.

**Download report** saves what the check found as a text file, with the printer's details first, to attach to an issue. **Download support bundle** downloads `valve-oil-support-<printer>-<time>.zip`, the same bundle **Report a problem** on the Updates screen downloads: the printer's logs, Valve Oil's update output, and the last check's report. See [Reporting a problem](troubleshooting.md#reporting-a-problem). The check needs a Valve Oil release that has it; on an older one, the card says to update Valve Oil.

## What the installer does

- **Installs the build in `~/vo-valve-oil-ui`** and has the printer's web server serve it at `/valve-oil/`, next to Fluidd. It reaches Klipper through the same Moonraker connection Fluidd uses, so there's nothing to set up.
- **Updates replace the whole build.** Open Valve Oil UI tabs switch to the new version by themselves.
- **It's served only together with this project's [Fluidd](fluidd.md).** If the installer doesn't recognize QIDI's web server configuration for Fluidd, or `~/vo-valve-oil-ui` already holds files the installer didn't put there, it leaves them alone and `/valve-oil/` isn't served.

## Undoing it

Uninstalling, over SSH or with **Uninstall** on the [Updates](#updates) screen, removes the `/valve-oil/` route and `~/vo-valve-oil-ui`, and puts QIDI's Fluidd back.
